# 154-Day Aircraft/Crew Recovery — Supervisor Review Brief

**Planning horizon:** 25 October 2026–27 March 2027 inclusive (154 days)  
**Purpose:** Review a complete-horizon research prototype, identify data blockers, and agree the next validation criteria.  
**Status:** Preliminary analytical output only; not an operationally approved airline recovery plan.

## 1. Existing full-horizon baseline

These are historical baseline figures, not a newly executed run. Re-run the current branch before labelling them as the latest result.

## 2. What the model currently does

- Builds a complete-horizon operate/cancel plan from the prepared schedule.
- Selects from the supplied validated crew-pairing columns and generated aircraft route arcs, depending on run configuration.
- Checks crew-pairing coverage, aircraft route continuity, equipment compatibility as represented in the inputs, and MGT where rules are available.
- Exports flight-level decisions, schedule reconciliation, selected pairings, aircraft rotations, validation findings, and a summary.

## 3. Important limitations to disclose

- Long-horizon output is heuristic, not a globally optimal solution.
- The current native model does not jointly optimize flight-copy retiming as part of the MILP.
- Existing pairing coverage is incomplete; a flight without a candidate may be left unoperated even when a new legal pairing could recover it.
- LINE_n aircraft are inferred/provisional resources, not confirmed tail registrations and starting states.
- Nine MGT rules were unresolved in the recorded baseline. These must remain visible blockers, not be silently guessed.
- The objective does not yet include every approved operating-cost term, roster/set decisions, or a globally complete pairing-generation domain.

## 4. Questions for the supervisor / data owner

Please confirm or provide the authoritative source for each item:

1. **MGT rules:** What are the approved minimum ground times by station, arriving equipment/subfleet, and any date/operating-condition exceptions? Who owns the rulebook?
2. **Aircraft starting states:** Actual tail registrations, starting station, available-from time, maintenance/unavailability, and the mapping from each flight equipment code (320, 32N, 32P, 32Q) to eligible tails.
3. **Crew pairing domain:** Is the supplied pairing report the complete set of approved pairings, or may the model generate new pairings? What duty/rest/positioning rules and crew qualifications must govern generated pairings?
4. **Recovery policy:** Is cancellation minimization strictly the first priority? What are the permitted retiming increments and maximum delay, and are cancellations, delay minutes, swaps, positioning, and passenger impacts ranked in a specified order?
5. **Cost objective:** Approved values for cancellation, retiming/delay, aircraft operating cost, crew duty cost, positioning, and any other required terms.
6. **Acceptance criteria:** What minimum schedule coverage, zero-tolerance hard-constraint checks, runtime, and evidence are required before a result is considered a valid demonstration?

## 5. Review evidence to bring

Use the files generated by the latest run, rather than screenshots alone:

- summary.json and horizon_summary.json
- flight_decisions.csv — every flight's operate/cancel decision and reason
- schedule_reconciliation.csv — original versus recovered times
- selected_pairings.csv — crew-pairing selections
- aircraft_rotations.csv — aircraft route assignments
- aircraft_validation_violations.csv and pairing-validation output
- the run log and scenario manifest, if a disruption scenario was tested

For the meeting, show the coverage and blocker counts first, then one or two traceable examples of a recovered route/pairing and one example that remains blocked. Do not describe the prototype as operationally feasible while unresolved MGT or unverified tail-state assumptions remain.

## 6. Definition of a successful next iteration

The next iteration should use supervisor-approved source data, produce a reproducible 154-day output, preserve all hard legal/operational constraints, explain every flight left unoperated, and report exactly which checks passed or failed. Any retiming benefit must be demonstrated by comparing the baseline and retimed outputs using the same inputs and horizon.
